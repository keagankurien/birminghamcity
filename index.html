Content audit · Off-season 2026

The best thing on these feeds went everywhere.

A channel by channel read of Birmingham City's current output, and the small number of things I'd still raise.

Everything here comes from public posts. It's an outside reading. I've no view of the calendar, the resourcing, the partner obligations, or why any single post went out the way it did. Where something looks like a gap, it may well be a decision I can't see from here.
01 — Snapshot

What the feeds sound like.

A Championship club posted a clip of one of its players sliding stones across a table and it did 1.1 million likes. No badge in shot doing the work. No caption explaining the reference. Paik, a format the entire planet already knew, and a top comment that outperformed most club posts on its own.

That's the thing worth naming first, because it explains the rest. This account is quick on formats and it lets players be people inside them. The Netflix documentary bit ran on the Dael Fry unveiling while the format was still current. Mecha Chameleon has been and gone. Whoever is watching what's moving is watching properly, and the turnaround is fast enough to catch things before they curdle.

The other half of it is knowing when to stop. The humour is picked rather than constant, and there's a straight, sober register for the days that need one. Plenty of clubs choose a lane and stay in it. The ones that try both usually get the timing wrong.

02 — The audit

Channel by channel.

Tap any channel to open it. Linked cards go to the post being talked about.

IG
Instagram
The shop window
+
Current use

Kit campaigns, matchday graphics, fan portraits, signings. The channel where the club looks most like itself.

What works

The kit photography earns saves, which is the number worth watching here, because a save is someone deciding to come back to it. The Penguin flat-lay is the standard the rest of the feed gets measured against, and it's a high one.

The fan portrait pulled the most comments of anything I looked at. That's not a coincidence. Put a supporter in frame and the supporters turn up.

The Dael Fry unveiling is the clearest recent example of how fast this account moves. It ran the Netflix documentary format — player in a chair, caption over the top, the joke doing the work — on a signing announcement, while the format was still current. I had that written down as something to suggest. They'd already shipped it.

What feels underused

The ordinary Tuesday. The voice that makes the account distinctive shows up on the big days and goes quiet between them, and the day-to-day graphics don't carry it the way the campaign work does. The feed has two speeds and you can see the join.

The fan portrait works and it happens when a good image happens to exist. As a strand people could expect, it'd do more.

X
X / Twitter
Voice, not speed
+
Current use

Team news, results, kit reveals, reactive moments, the odd bit of needle.

What works

Copy discipline. Might be too hot under a sun-bleached mural is the whole account at its best. Four words, trusts you to get it, doesn't explain itself. The posts that land don't over-write.

I'd flagged the in-game update volume as thin. I don't think that any more. Supporters get scores from FotMob and SofaScore notifications before a club account can type them, and they're watching the stream anyway. Chelsea's live cadence works because a global audience is refreshing their timeline for it; that isn't the same job. Posting less in-game and better around it looks like a call someone made on purpose, not a gap.

What feels underused

Between fixtures the copy gets longer and more functional, which is the opposite of what makes the account good. X moves posts through replies and quotes, so the ones that leave a door open get dragged into other people's timelines. The short, dry register that works on matchday mostly stays there.

Somewhere in the feed there's a reply to a Villa fan mid-rant that just says bro are u ok? I couldn't find it again to link it, which is its own small problem. It's still the line I kept thinking about while writing this, and it's the account at its best: four words, no defensiveness, and it treats a bloke shouting into a comment box like a person rather than a moderation issue.

TT
TikTok
The strongest channel they have
+
Current use

Player personality, skill clips, matchday reaction, creator collabs. Made for TikTok rather than dumped on it.

What works

7.3M likes against 332K followers. The content is travelling well beyond the people who already follow, which is the only real reason to be on the platform.

The Squid Game clip is the best thing this club has ever posted on any channel. 1.1M likes, 70.9K saves, 4,449 comments. Paik at a table with a set of stones, a format the whole planet recognised, and no need to explain the joke to anyone. The top comment did 112.8K likes on its own, which is more than most club posts manage. That video has out-performed the entire rest of the feed by an order of magnitude and it cost a table, a backdrop and half an hour.

The pinned grid tells the same story. 9.6M, 9.7M and 11.5M views sitting at the top of the profile. This isn't a channel that gets lucky occasionally.

Then there's the creator work. The BNFreestyle collab put the stadium in front of an audience that had no reason to care about Blues beforehand. Mid-size creators, not names. That's recruitment rather than content, and it's the cheapest kind going.

What feels underused

The Squid Game post proves something the rest of the feed doesn't act on often enough. It worked because it borrowed a format everyone already knew and let a player be a person inside it. No club branding doing the heavy lifting, no explanation. That's a repeatable idea, not a fluke, and it doesn't need a global TV phenomenon to work — only a format people recognise.

Blue Rising, The POWERHOUSE and Built In Birmingham are all named on the profile, but the TikTok cuts are thin next to what's on YouTube. The show has an audience; the vertical clips aren't yet the front door to it.

f
Facebook
The biggest room, quietest voice
+
Current use

Results, kit, commercial posts, mostly carried across from elsewhere.

What works

It's the largest audience the club has, and the comment threads are alive in a way they aren't everywhere else. Reach here comes through shares into personal feeds, and the posts people share tend to be the warm ones. Heritage, faces, anything that says look at this rather than here is the news.

What feels underused

Cross-posting isn't a sin. Cross-posting everything means the biggest audience the club has gets a version of the club that was written for someone else, in a room where the sharing behaviour is completely different.

This is also the audience most likely to remember 150 years of it. Heritage travels here in a way it doesn't on TikTok, and it turns up occasionally rather than as a habit.

YT
YouTube
A proper show, free to watch
+
Current use

Blue Rising, an episodic magazine show, plus long-form films and campaign work.

What works

Blue Rising is the thing. Episode 15 premiered in November and has done 403,000 views. Kerys Harrop as a guest, extended highlights from the 150th anniversary game against Norwich and the Women's Second City Derby, Lee Carsley on the England Under-21s, and On Your Shed Son returning as a segment.

Two things there matter more than the view count. It's numbered, which means people know where they are in it. And it has returning segments, which only makes sense if an audience is expecting them. Most Championship clubs upload highlights and press conferences. This is a show, and it's free.

Booking a Blues Women legend as a guest on the main channel show is the other thing worth naming. It treats the women's side as part of the club's story rather than a separate feed to be serviced.

What feels underused

A show this good should be recruiting on the platforms where people find things. The vertical channels carry a Blue Rising playlist but not much of the show itself, and a 400,000-view episode is a lot of material to leave sitting in one place.

Thumbnails are the small one. Different fonts, different crops, no family resemblance down the grid, which makes an episodic series harder to recognise in a sidebar. That's a template rather than a strategy question.

03 — Where there's room

One note, two openings.

This list started longer. Most of it went, because the club had already done it.

/ 01

Mecha Chameleon: hide it worse

Blues have run this one. So has half the league. The format is a small white figure hidden somewhere in frame and everyone hunts for it, and most versions treat it as a puzzle. Chelsea's is the clearest example of the problem: the figure is hidden so well that finding it becomes work, and work isn't funny.

The laugh is in the placement, not the difficulty. Standing on the halfway line. Sat in the dugout with its legs crossed. Next to the kitman like it's staff. Creators worked this out ages ago, which is why their versions travel further than the clubs'. It's a decision made at the shoot, costs nothing, and it suits this account's sense of humour better than the puzzle version does.

Adjustment, not a pitch
/ 02

Creators, as a programme

BNFreestyle worked because the club went to someone else's audience instead of waiting for it. Mid-size creators are the right call. Big enough to bring a crowd, small enough to actually turn up and enjoy it, and they make things that look like their own channel rather than an ad.

One-offs are a tactic. A standing slot with a handful of them is a route into audiences that have no reason to know Blues exist. Of everything on this page, it's the one I'd call a genuine gap rather than a preference.

Extends: BNFreestyle
/ 03

Blue Rising, travelling further

A free, numbered show doing 400,000 views an episode is the biggest asset on any of these channels, and the vertical feeds carry almost none of it. Whether that's rights, edit time or someone's already got it on a list, I can't see from here.

Raising it anyway, because it's the widest gap between what exists and how many people meet it.

Extends: Blue Rising
Where this read was wrong

Three things I had down as gaps in May and don't any more, listed together because the corrections say more about the club than the original notes did.

In-game cadence on X. I'd marked the volume as thin. Supporters get scores from FotMob and SofaScore before a club account can type them, and they're watching the stream anyway. Posting less in-game is a call someone made, not an oversight.

Formats. I had the Netflix documentary bit written down as a suggestion. It ran on the Dael Fry unveiling before I'd finished the sentence. Same with Mecha Chameleon.

Recurring series. I'd written that nothing on TikTok had a name or a slot. There are three named playlists on the profile and a numbered show on YouTube with returning segments.

None of those corrections came from the feeds alone. They came from looking harder, or from being told.

What I'd want to learn next.

This is an off-season read and that's the honest limit of it. A proper audit runs a season, and half the things I'd flagged in May turned out to be decisions rather than gaps. The in-game cadence on X. The formats I thought were missing and weren't. Every one of those corrections came from looking harder or being told, and none came from the feeds alone.

Which is the actual argument for being in the room. I can read output from outside and I've shown that here, including the parts where it caught me out. What I can't see is the calendar, the constraints, or what a matchday costs to run. I'd like to, and I'd rather earn that by being useful on one than by writing another page about it.
