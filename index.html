External Social Media Audit · May 2026 · Based on publicly visible posts and engagement signals

Birmingham City FC

Birmingham City are operating with a stronger brand narrative than at most points in recent memory. New ownership, a stadium project with the potential to transform East Birmingham, a 150th anniversary season, and a squad with genuine international reach. The social channels reflect some of that ambition, particularly on kit campaigns and fan-led content, but not consistently. The gap between BCFC's strongest social output and its average output is what this audit is concerned with.

Instagram
Primary brand channel · 327,000 followers
+
Performance
Based on the posts reviewed, the strongest content earns serious engagement. The Penguin re-release: 8,934 likes and 2,438 saves. The Championship 26/27 fixture graphic: 9,355 likes and 685 saves. The fan portrait: 1,555 likes and 32 comments, the highest comment count among the posts sampled. Saves and comments are stronger indicators of content quality than likes alone.
Brand voice
At its best, short and specific. "It is well hot" on a sun-bleached mural image is the right register: it trusts the audience and says less than it could. The gap is that this voice shows up on the big days and disappears the rest of the week.
Visual identity
Royal blue is well-established as the visual anchor. Kit photography is genuinely strong, the Penguin flat-lay is the standard. Day-to-day graphics do not consistently meet it. The feed has two speeds and the gap between them is visible.
What's working
Kit photography earns saves. Fan content earns comments. Campaign output stands up against top-flight clubs.
What could improve
Reels frequency is inconsistent. Day-to-day graphics fall below campaign standard. Fan stories appear occasionally, not as a regular, named format.
Fix
Set a brief for three things: a weekly fan story format, a consistent Reels schedule, and a visual template for international call-up content. None require additional budget. They require editorial discipline and a standing content brief.

X / Twitter
Matchday and reactive channel · highest impression volume
+
Performance
Impression volume is the most visible signal on X from the posts reviewed. The Royal Blue mural post reached 2,356 likes and 32 shares. The "Together" season graphic hit 173,000 impressions. The Paik World Cup post earned 1,612 likes. These figures suggest the content is reaching beyond the immediate follower base on stronger posts, though without full analytics access the precise reach breakdown is not confirmed.
Brand voice
Three words on the right image outperforms three sentences on the wrong one every time. "It is well hot" is the standard. The posts that land do not explain themselves. Between the big moments, the copy gets longer, more functional, and less distinctive.
Content themes
Match updates · squad news · results · reactive cultural moments · kit reveals · Paik Seung-Ho World Cup content.
Gap
Personality-led content and reactive cultural commentary appear largely absent outside of matchday, based on the posts reviewed. Clubs that tend to perform well on X maintain a more consistent voice across the week rather than concentrating energy on matchday alone. The copy discipline evident in the strongest posts does not consistently carry through the full week.
Opportunity
One non-matchday post per day with a standing brief to be short, specific, and in voice. The "Birmingham, on the rise" positioning gives the account a clear point of view to write from, which is not yet being used consistently between fixtures.
Fix
Brief the content team on a daily non-matchday post with a two-line limit. No exceptions. The template should be: observation or reaction, one emoji, no explanation. Review weekly against the impression data to see what format is pulling.

TikTok
Fastest-growing channel · 323K followers · 6.8M total likes · 21:1 ratio
+
Performance
The 21:1 likes-to-follower ratio is a strong engagement signal relative to the other channels in the portfolio. The BNFreestyle stadium collab earned 6,574 likes and 93 shares. This ratio suggests content is reaching beyond the existing follower base with some regularity, which is an encouraging indicator for a channel at this stage of growth.
Brand voice
Looser, more human, and more platform-native than any other channel in the portfolio. Player personality content lands here in a way it does not on Instagram. The club appears to understand TikTok as a distinct format rather than treating it as a repost destination.
Content themes
Player Q&As, skill clips, matchday reaction, creator collabs. The BNFreestyle collab showed what happens when the stadium is used as a canvas for external creators. The audience it reached had no prior BCFC interest. That is audience acquisition, not just content.
Gap
No recurring weekly series format is visible from the posts reviewed. Without a consistent content strand, growth is more likely to come in engagement spikes rather than sustained follower accumulation. Thumbnail identity also appears inconsistent, which may reduce click-through in the For You feed. Both are likely addressable without additional budget.
Opportunity
A named weekly format, consistent posting day, and a thumbnail template system would provide a more reliable structure for follower growth. Extending the creator collab programme beyond one-offs, along the lines of the BNFreestyle approach, appears to be a low-cost route to reaching audiences outside the existing fanbase.
Fix
Consider commissioning regular creator collabs using the BNFreestyle approach as a reference point. Establishing a consistent posting rhythm and a thumbnail template system in the club colours are production-level decisions that do not require additional creative resource.

Facebook
Largest raw audience · 420,000 followers · heritage and commercial reach
+
Performance
420,000 followers is the largest raw audience across the channels reviewed. Organic reach on Facebook has declined industry-wide, so the follower count reflects brand presence more than active engagement rate. The commercial value here is likely sponsor visibility to the club's broadest demographic rather than content performance in the traditional sense.
Brand voice
Broadcast-led. The club posts, supporters respond, the club does not re-enter the conversation. Comment threads are active and passionate, which makes the one-way communication more noticeable.
Content themes
Match results, kit announcements, commercial content. Heritage content appears occasionally and performs well when it does. Longer video is better suited to this platform than any other in the portfolio but is not being used strategically.
Gap
Based on the posts reviewed, content appears largely cross-posted from X and Instagram rather than written for Facebook's specific audience. The older supporter demographic that Facebook tends to skew towards does not appear to be served with heritage storytelling, long-form video, or community content built with that audience in mind.
Opportunity
A monthly heritage feature, archive photography, legendary players, major moments from 150 years of history, costs almost nothing to produce and is exactly what this audience wants. Longer-form video should be Facebook-first, not repurposed from YouTube.
Fix
Briefing one heritage post per month specifically for Facebook would be a low-cost starting point. Responding to the top comments on each post within 24 hours is a minimal but visible signal that the audience is actively managed, which has value both for community trust and for demonstrating engagement to commercial partners.
YouTube
Brand film and long-form channel
+
Performance
YouTube is structurally constrained by the Blues+ paywall, which limits the volume of free content available to build algorithmic momentum. Where flagship content exists, the quality is high. The 150th anniversary film and the LA-shot Global kit campaign would hold up against top-flight output. The problem is frequency, not quality.
Brand voice
Documentary and editorial when given the space. The best content on the channel tells a story rather than just covering an event. That instinct needs a consistent format to live in.
Visual identity
Thumbnail consistency is weak. Different fonts, compositions, and colour treatments across the channel reduce brand recognition in search and in the recommendation feed. A single thumbnail template system would fix this immediately.
Gap
No recurring series to drive habitual viewership. Between flagship films, the channel does not give audiences a reason to return on a fixed schedule. The paywall is a structural constraint, but the editorial gap is a strategic choice that can be changed.
Opportunity
The new stadium represents a significant long-form content opportunity. A club documenting its own build, from planning through construction to opening day, would have a compelling PR narrative and years of milestone content. Few Championship clubs have a comparable story to tell. Building the audience now, before the build begins, would maximise the momentum when the bigger milestones arrive.
Fix
A thumbnail template system applied consistently across the channel would improve discoverability at minimal cost. A recurring monthly free series, whether behind-the-scenes, academy features, or matchday documentaries, would give the channel a more reliable content rhythm alongside the paywalled material.
LinkedIn
B2B and commercial channel · ~24,000 followers
+
Performance
Around 24,000 followers, which is below the potential for a club with this commercial narrative. The December 2025 Social Media Creator vacancy post was amplified on X and reached 27,800 impressions, showing that the right LinkedIn content does travel when it connects with a relevant audience.
Brand voice
Professional but underused based on the public-facing posts reviewed. No consistent editorial programme around the transformation narrative that appears to be driving the wider brand. The Knighthead story, the Sports Quarter vision, and the new stadium plans represent comparatively strong B2B content for a Championship club. From what is publicly visible, none of it is being told in a structured way on this channel.
Content themes
Hiring posts, commercial announcements, occasional infrastructure news. The channel functions as a job board and press release feed rather than as a commercial brand platform.
Gap
No executive thought leadership or strategic narrative is visible from the public posts reviewed. The transformation story that appears to drive the broader brand is largely absent on this channel. It is reasonable to assume that potential sponsors and commercial partners may research a club's LinkedIn presence before entering conversations, making this an underdeveloped commercial touchpoint.
Opportunity
Monthly posts from club leadership on the stadium project, the Sports Quarter development, and the Knighthead vision could generate sponsor interest before formal conversations begin. Few Championship clubs appear to be using LinkedIn in this way, which suggests a potential early-mover advantage, though the commercial impact would depend on execution and targeting.
Fix
Briefing club leadership on a small number of LinkedIn posts per year tied to stadium and Sports Quarter milestones would be a practical starting point. Assigning a content owner to the channel rather than treating it as a scheduling task would make a visible difference to the quality and consistency of what goes out.

Standards
The editorial standard this audit is measured against
  • Copy written for the platform it lives on. What works on X does not work on Instagram.
  • Supporter stories on a fixed weekly schedule, not published when a good image happens to exist
  • Kit and product photography that earns saves, not just likes
  • TikTok content built natively for TikTok with a consistent posting rhythm
  • International player content with a visual template that builds global reach between tournaments
  • LinkedIn and Facebook treated as distinct channels with their own briefs, not as overflow from other platforms
Recommendations
Four changes with the highest commercial and audience impact
01
Codify the short-copy standard into a standing brief. BCFC already knows what good looks like. The Penguin post, the Royal Blue mural, the "Together" graphic. Turn that into a written brief that governs every caption, every day of the week.
02
Commission a weekly supporter story series with a fixed format and name. The fan portrait post earned the highest comment count on the feed. Comments are where real community exists. A weekly series with a consistent format turns that into a reliable engagement and loyalty driver.
03
Build a visual template for international player content and use it every call-up window. BCFC have players representing Korea, Poland, and Japan at international level. Each one is a route into a fanbase that has no prior connection to a Championship club. A consistent format and posting cadence turns reactive coverage into a global audience strategy.
04
Begin the stadium documentary series now, not when the build starts. The planning stage, the community consultation, the renderings, the vision. A club that documents the full journey from planning to opening day could build a meaningfully larger audience before the stadium opens. That kind of audience growth, if sustained, would carry commercial value for naming rights conversations, kit sponsors, and local business partners.
Looking ahead
The new stadium changes the content ambition entirely
Powerhouse Stadium · 62,000 seats · East Birmingham
The stadium project is a major content asset with strong long-term potential. It is not yet being fully used.
Few Championship clubs have a comparable infrastructure story to tell. A club that tells it well, from community consultation through to opening day, has the basis for a documentary series, a compelling sponsorship narrative, and years of milestone content. Building the audience before the build begins gives that content far greater reach when the major moments arrive. LinkedIn is where the strategic and commercial story belongs. YouTube is where the documentary series lives. X handles the real-time milestones. The content framework should be in place well before the build starts.
The raw material is strong. A new stadium in planning, a 150th anniversary, a squad with genuine international interest, and a fanbase that engages when the content is right. Based on what is publicly visible, the channels are not yet converting that consistently into sustained social growth. That is the opportunity this audit is pointing at.
