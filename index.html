Social Media Audit · May 2026

Birmingham City FC

BCFC have a genuinely strong position right now. 150th anniversary, Knighthead ownership, a new stadium on the way. The channels are good when they are good. The Penguin kit post, the fan portrait, the Royal Blue mural. But the gap between that standard and what fills the feed in between is wide enough to notice. The story is right. The output needs to match it.

Instagram
Primary visual brand channel · 327,000 followers
+
Tone of voice
Confident and civic. Short captions that trust the image. At its best it sounds like someone who actually watches BCFC. Not a brand account going through the motions.
Visual style
Royal blue dominant. Clean flat-lay kit photography on big campaigns. Day-to-day graphic quality drops below that standard.
Content themes
Kit launches · matchday · fan portraits · international player features · 150th anniversary · Knighthead era content.
What's working
Kit photography earns saves. Fan content earns comments. Campaign output stands up against top-flight clubs.
What could improve
Reels frequency is inconsistent. Day-to-day graphics fall below campaign standard. Fan stories appear occasionally, not as a regular, named format.
Opportunity
A weekly fan story format. The data shows it outperforms almost everything else on the feed, build around it.

X / Twitter
Real-time matchday channel · news breaks here first
+
Tone of voice
Short and reactive at its best. Three words on the right image outperforms three sentences on the wrong one every time. The posts that land do not explain themselves.
Visual style
Inconsistent template quality across the week. Strong on the big moments, more functional in between.
Content themes
Match updates · squad news · results · reactive cultural moments · kit reveals · Paik Seung-Ho World Cup content.
What's working
Reactive instinct is sharp. Impressions data signals reach beyond the existing follower base when the copy lands.
What could improve
Copy gets longer and more functional between big moments. Personality-led banter and reactive humour, consistently high-performing on X, are largely absent.
Opportunity
Set one personality-led post per day as a standing brief, not match content. Build the habit of the voice, not just the information.

TikTok
Audience growth channel · 323K followers · 6.8M total likes
+
Tone of voice
Looser and more human than Instagram. Player personality content lands here in a way it doesn't anywhere else in the portfolio.
Visual style
Authenticity-first, appropriate for the platform. No consistent thumbnail identity in the feed, which limits discoverability.
Content themes
Player Q&As · skill clips · matchday reaction · creator collabs. Player personality content is where TikTok performs best for BCFC and there is no shortage of characters in the squad to build it around.
What's working
21:1 likes-per-follower ratio, the strongest engagement signal in the portfolio. Creator collabs (BNFreestyle) show real platform instinct. Content feels native, not repurposed.
What could improve
No recurring weekly series to build habitual return viewing. Inconsistent posting frequency limits follower growth despite high engagement signals.
Opportunity
A named weekly player feature would convert the strong engagement ratio into consistent follower growth. The audience is already there.
BNFreestyle collab, 6,574 likes · 93 shares 21:1 likes-per-follower ratio
Facebook
Largest raw audience · 420,000 followers
+
Tone of voice
Broadcast-led. Active fan comment culture exists, but the club's own participation in those threads is limited. It's talking at fans rather than with them.
Visual style
Cross-posted from other channels. No dedicated Facebook formatting or design approach for the platform's specific audience.
Content themes
Match results · kit and commercial announcements · occasional heritage content. Longer video performs better here than anywhere else.
What's working
Largest audience for commercial partner visibility. Supporter comment threads are genuinely active. Heritage content performs strongly with the demographic.
What could improve
No Facebook-native content strategy. The older supporter demographic is not being served with tailored storytelling or heritage-specific content.
Opportunity
150 years of history, archive footage, legendary players. Heritage content costs almost nothing to produce and lands well with this specific audience.
YouTube
Brand film and long-form channel
+
Tone of voice
Production quality on flagship content is high. The 150th anniversary film and the LA-shot Global kit campaign are genuinely strong brand filmmaking. They would hold up against top-flight output.
Visual style
Strong video quality. Thumbnail consistency is weak, reduces brand recognition across the feed and in YouTube search results.
Content themes
Kit launch films · player interviews · pre/post-match features · academy content · heritage and anniversary films.
What's working
Flagship brand films stand up against top-flight output. Player storytelling is well-resourced. The new stadium is a ready-made long-form documentary series.
What could improve
Blues+ paywall limits free content volume. No recurring series to drive habitual viewership. Thumbnail system is inconsistent and reduces discoverability.
Opportunity
A season-long documentary built around the new stadium story and the 150th anniversary would be the most-watched content BCFC has ever published. The narrative material is already there.
LinkedIn
B2B and commercial channel · ~24,000 followers
+
Tone of voice
Professional but lightly maintained. No consistent editorial programme around the transformation narrative that's driving the wider brand.
Visual style
Clean and professional. It does not embarrass the club's commercial ambitions. It does not actively advance them either.
Content themes
Hiring posts · commercial announcements · occasional infrastructure news. Knighthead and Sports Quarter vision are largely absent.

Monthly posts from club leadership on the stadium and Sports Quarter vision. This story earns sponsors before the build even begins.

Standards
What good looks like
  • Short copy that earns attention. Three words on the right image beats three sentences on the wrong one.
  • Photography that treats the subject seriously, not just documents it
  • TikTok content built for TikTok, not lifted from Instagram
  • Supporter stories published consistently, not just when there is a good photo available
  • International call-up content with a consistent format that builds global reach week on week
  • Content written specifically for Facebook and LinkedIn, not the same post on every channel
Recommendations
Four practical moves
01
Set short copy as the daily default. The posts that prove it works are already on the feed. That is the bar.
02
Launch a named weekly fan story format. The 32-comment fan portrait shows the audience appetite is real. Build a format around it.
03
Give international call-up content a consistent visual format. A recurring template builds global followers week by week rather than only when a tournament comes around.
04
Start the new stadium story on LinkedIn and YouTube now. The content asset already exists. The audience needs to be built before the planning headlines peak.
Looking ahead
The new stadium changes the content ambition entirely
Powerhouse Stadium · 62,000 seats · East Birmingham
A new stadium is not just a venue announcement. It is a decade of content.
A club documenting its own build from planning through construction to opening day has a ready-made documentary series, a global PR story, and years of milestone content. The channels should be building that audience now, before the ground breaks. When the stadium opens, the goal is hundreds of thousands of people who were there for the whole journey. That does not happen by accident. It starts on LinkedIn, YouTube, and X.
The best BCFC content already travels. The Penguin kit, the fan portrait, the Royal Blue mural post. The task is making that the standard across the whole week, not just the big days.
